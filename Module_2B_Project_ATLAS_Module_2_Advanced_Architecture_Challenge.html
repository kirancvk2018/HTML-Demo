Project ATLAS: Advanced Enterprise Architecture Challenge

Module 2 · Scenario-based architecture assessment · Higher difficulty than the foundation quiz

Challenge brief

GlobalTech Manufacturing wants to extend Project ATLAS from a pilot used by two engineering teams to a shared service for 8,000 engineers across 12 countries. ATLAS helps engineers understand legacy code, assess change impact, draft tests and retrieve engineering knowledge.

Current estate

  • GitHub Enterprise for source code
  • Jira for work items and incidents
  • Confluence for architecture and runbooks
  • Three internal systems expose APIs; two have inconsistent schemas

Business constraints

  • Some engineering data must remain in its designated region
  • Peak demand is 1,200 concurrent users
  • Critical change recommendations require engineer approval
  • Initial rollout must integrate with existing identity and audit services

Quality targets

  • 95% of standard requests should return within 12 seconds
  • Availability target: 99.9% monthly
  • Every answer must identify its evidence sources
  • Untrusted repository content must not override system policy

Architecture budget

  • Use managed services where they reduce operational burden
  • Avoid duplicating enterprise capabilities without a clear reason
  • Support phased delivery and measurable acceptance criteria
Your role: You are the solution architect. Choose an architecture that satisfies the constraints, explain the trade-offs, and identify what must be validated before production. There may be more than one defensible design, but some options violate explicit requirements.

Part A — Architecture judgement

10 questions · Select the strongest answer, not merely a technically possible one.
Advanced · 10 points

Part B — Choose an architecture pattern

For each requirement, select the most appropriate primary pattern. Consider determinism, variability, governance and operational complexity.

Part C — Design decisions and trade-offs

Write concise architect-level responses. These are assessed with a review checklist rather than an automated score.

1. Define the system boundary

Identify what ATLAS owns, what remains in enterprise systems, and where trust/security boundaries sit.

2. Recommend a Claude integration and orchestration approach

Explain how the application invokes the model, coordinates specialist capabilities and handles tools. Avoid treating every task as an autonomous agent task.

3. Write one Architecture Decision Record (ADR)

State the decision, context, alternatives considered, rationale, consequences and validation required.

4. Define measurable acceptance criteria

Provide at least four testable criteria covering response time, availability, evidence traceability, security or human approval.

Architect review checklist
  • Boundaries and ownership are explicit; GitHub/Jira/Confluence remain systems of record.
  • Functional and non-functional requirements are distinguished and measurable.
  • Pattern selection is justified per use case; deterministic flows are preferred where steps are known.
  • Claude is accessed through a controlled integration layer; model choice is not confused with orchestration.
  • Tool access is least-privilege, scoped and auditable; retrieved content is treated as untrusted data.
  • Regional data constraints, identity, audit and human approval are represented in the design.
  • Trade-offs, failure modes, assumptions and unresolved validation items are documented in ADRs.
