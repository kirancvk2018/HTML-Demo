01 · PURPOSE

Why this module exists

BMad structures collaboration between people and AI tools across planning and implementation. This module is about the planning half — turning business intent into implementation context that a development team can act on without guessing.

The current official BMad documentation describes a workflow organized around analysis (optional), planning, solutioning, and implementation — with the amount of process adjusted to the size and uncertainty of the change.

The objective of this module is not to treat AI output as authoritative. It is to use structured workflows and human decisions to produce clearer implementation context.

What "planning" means in BMad

  • Progressively clarifying business intent into requirements
  • Turning requirements into a concise specification
  • Breaking specifications into epics, stories, and tasks
  • Keeping every artifact traceable back to the original problem
02 · OVERVIEW

The four phases

BMad — Breakthrough Method for Agile AI-Driven Development — is a set of named skills usable with AI coding tools. Thinking/planning and implementation are distinguished, and either can be used independently. Select a phase to see what it covers.

Proportional by design

The amount of planning is not fixed. A small, well-understood change can go straight to implementation. A larger product or feature can move through deeper planning and solutioning. Matching process to change size is a core practical principle, covered further in Section 3.

03 · CORE PRINCIPLES

Three ideas that govern everything else

Every later section — elicitation, specs, decomposition, review — is an application of these three principles.

Progressive elaboration

The workflow moves from intent toward increasingly precise implementation context. The goal is to add the information needed for the next decision, not to produce documentation for its own sake.

Intent → ambiguity resolved → requirements captured → specification drafted → stories broken out → implementation context handed off → results reviewed and fed back.

Match process to change size

A small, clear change does not require a large planning exercise. A large feature, new product, or ambiguous business problem benefits from more analysis and planning before implementation begins. Over- and under-planning are both treated as failure modes — see Section 14.

Human–AI collaboration

BMad amplifies human decision-making rather than replacing it. People supply business intent, constraints, decisions, and approvals. AI assists with questioning, structuring, drafting, analysis, review, and implementation activities — it does not make the business call.

04 · BUSINESS REQUIREMENTS

Capturing business requirements

Before AI is asked to produce detailed stories or code, the team establishes the problem, users, outcome, and constraints. AI can surface missing information — but the business owner confirms actual intent.

PROBLEM
What business or user problem is being addressed?
USERS / STAKEHOLDERS
Who experiences the problem or depends on the capability?
OUTCOME
What result should the organization or user achieve?
SCOPE
What is included, and what is explicitly outside the current change?
CONSTRAINTS
Business, technical, regulatory, operational, or organizational limits that matter.
SUCCESS
How will the team know the intended outcome has been achieved?
05 · DOMAIN MODELING

Modeling business domains and capabilities

Domain modeling gives the team a shared vocabulary and identifies the capabilities the system must support. AI can propose candidate concepts and relationships — domain experts validate terminology and rules.

DOMAIN
The business area or problem space being addressed.
ACTORS
Users, roles, organizations, or external systems that interact with the domain.
BUSINESS CONCEPTS
Important entities, terms, states, and relationships.
CAPABILITIES
What the organization or system must be able to do.
RULES
Business policies or constraints that affect behavior.
DEPENDENCIES
Other capabilities, systems, data, or processes required for the outcome.

A useful domain model is understandable to both business and technical stakeholders — it is a shared reference, not a technical artifact handed down to the business side.

06 · ELICITATION

AI-assisted requirement elicitation

AI acts as a structured questioning and analysis assistant. Select a pattern to see how it's used.

The safe elicitation loop

source information → AI questions → stakeholder answers → revised requirement → human review

AI should not silently fill business gaps with invented assumptions. Every gap it identifies is routed back to a human for an answer.

07 · DECOMPOSITION

Vision, epics, and user stories

Product vision, epics, and stories progressively translate intent into implementable work, each tier narrower and more concrete than the last.

VISION
Product vision
Connects the problem, target users, and desired outcome — a higher-level reference for everything planned afterward.
EPIC
Epic
A substantial capability or body of work that can be decomposed into smaller deliverables. Connects product intent to implementable work.
Example epic: Self-service IT request management
STORY
User story
A small, user- or stakeholder-centered outcome. Common structure: As a [user/role], I want [capability], so that [benefit].
As an employee, I want to submit an IT service request so that the service desk can process my need.

What makes a decomposition good

  • Each story represents a coherent outcome
  • The story is small enough for the team's implementation workflow
  • Acceptance criteria make expected behavior testable
  • Dependencies and non-functional requirements survive decomposition
  • Stories retain a link back to the requirement or capability they came from
08 · SPECIFICATION

The implementation-ready specification

bmad-spec produces a concise specification contract that locks the "what" before implementation, across five core fields. Implementation-ready does not mean every line of code is specified — it means intent and constraints are sufficient. Expand each field.

09 · BACKLOG

Task decomposition and backlog creation

Once requirements and specifications are clear, work is decomposed further to reduce ambiguity and make each piece independently understandable.

CAPABILITY
The business or product capability being delivered.
EPIC
A larger coherent body of work.
STORY
A user/stakeholder-centered increment of behavior.
TASK
A concrete implementation or supporting activity required to complete a story.
TEST / VALIDATION
Activities required to demonstrate acceptance conditions and quality expectations are met.
BMad's current workflow map describes Story Breakdown as the workflow used to break work into stories and track it. bmad-spec can optionally produce an ordered stories.yaml for implementing an epic across several sessions.
10 · VALIDATION

Validation and refinement using AI

AI is used for review as well as generation: checking whether a proposed artifact is consistent with its source requirement, and whether gaps remain.

CONSISTENCY
Does the story/specification agree with the approved business requirement?
COMPLETENESS
Are important behaviors, constraints, dependencies, or failure cases missing?
TESTABILITY
Can acceptance criteria be objectively validated?
CONTRADICTIONS
Does the artifact conflict with another requirement or constraint?
SCOPE
Has the proposed work introduced unnecessary functionality?
IMPLEMENTATION READINESS
Does the team have enough context to begin without guessing about important intent?

A useful review prompt requires the AI to separate facts supplied in the source material from assumptions and proposed improvements. Human review remains necessary for business decisions, architecture, security, and other consequential judgments.

11 · LIVING SPECIFICATIONS

Maintaining living specifications

A living specification is maintained as the product evolves — it does not go stale after the first development cycle.

CHANGE RECONCILIATION
When an approved requirement changes, identify and update affected specification elements.
CONFLICT DETECTION
Surface contradictions between the changed requirement and existing artifacts before implementation.
TRACEABILITY
Keep relationships between requirements, stories, implementation, and tests understandable.
VERSION CONTROL
Maintain specifications using the organization's normal document/version-control practices.
CONTEXT MANAGEMENT
Keep only relevant, current project information in the implementation context.

The objective is alignment: the specification should describe the current intended behavior, not historical intent.

    13 · TRAINING EXAMPLE

    Enterprise IT Service Request Portal

    An illustrative case study for training purposes only — not a claim about any real organization's system.

    Business problem: Employees need a consistent way to submit IT service requests and track their status.

    CAPABILITY

    Submit and track an IT service request.

    ACTORS

    Employee/requester, service desk, and supporting resolver teams.

    EPIC

    Self-service IT request management.

    ILLUSTRATIVE STORY

    As an employee, I want to submit an IT service request so that the service desk can process my need.

    SPECIFICATION FOCUS

    Capture required request information, establish status behavior, define constraints, identify the success signal.

    AI ROLE vs HUMAN ROLE

    AI: clarification questions, candidate stories, edge cases, consistency review.
    Human: service-management rules, ownership, security, priorities, acceptance decisions.

    The example deliberately avoids inventing service-level targets, security policies, integrations, or workflows. Those values must come from the actual organization's requirements.
    14 · COMMON MISTAKES

    Common BMad / AI planning mistakes

    Each of these undermines one of the three core principles from Section 3.

    Starting too early
    The team asks AI to code before business intent and important constraints are clear.
    Generated ≠ approved
    AI's proposed assumption is treated as an approved business decision.
    Over-planning
    The team creates unnecessary artifacts for a small, well-understood change.
    Under-planning
    A large or ambiguous initiative goes straight to implementation without enough context.
    Lost traceability
    Stories or tasks can no longer be connected back to the capability or requirement.
    Stale specifications
    Implementation changes but the specification remains unchanged.
    Over-prescriptive specs
    The specification dictates implementation details that don't need to be fixed at the requirements level.

    16 · TAKEAWAYS

    Key learning takeaways

    The essentials to carry out of this module.

    BMad provides structured AI-assisted workflows for thinking, planning, solutioning, and implementation.
    The amount of process should match the size, complexity, and uncertainty of the change.
    Business requirements should be clarified before AI is asked to generate downstream artifacts.
    Domain and capability modeling creates shared vocabulary and reduces ambiguity.
    Product vision, epics, stories, and tasks progressively translate intent into implementable work.
    The current BMad specification contract centers on Why, Capabilities, Constraints, Non-goals, and Success signal.
    AI can generate and critique artifacts, but approved business intent and consequential decisions remain human responsibilities.
    Living specifications keep requirements and implementation context aligned as the product changes.
